# Voice conversation

The user turns voice on, sees the live caption while speaking, hears a spoken
reply, and can stop the reply from the reticle or interrupt it by speaking
again. Turning voice off closes the microphone and the recognizer.

## Sub-features

- `voice-on` opens the microphone and moves through Starting to Listening.
- `voice-caption` shows the partial transcript under `Hearing you`, then
  commits it as a user turn.
- `voice-reply` speaks each reply sentence in the applied voice and returns to
  Listening.
- `voice-stop` stops a spoken reply from the reticle.
- `voice-barge-in` interrupts a spoken reply when the user starts speaking.
- `voice-off` closes the microphone and the recognizer connection.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Tap the reticle (`Start talking`) or the header's `Voice off` button.
- Speak. Tap the reticle (`Stop`) while Thalvi speaks, or just talk over it.
- Tap `Voice on` or the reticle (`End voice`) to finish.

## Driving it with thalvi-verify

Preconditions:

- Baseline from `features/README.md`, page at `idle`, voice off.

Steps:

- **Voice on.** Queue a turn, then tap the reticle.
  - Run `$V say $R "What should I cook with leftover rice"`,
    `$V ab $R click 'button[aria-label="Start talking"]'` and
    `$V state $R listening`.
  - `button.o-voice` has `aria-pressed="true"`, and `R/fakes.jsonl` has
    `asr_open` with `"mode": "ENDPOINTING", "encoding": "PCM_24KHZ"`.
- **Caption and turn.** The microphone clip's speech burst becomes the turn
  within about 9 s.
  - Run `$V ab $R wait --fn '[...document.querySelectorAll(".o-turns .o-user")].some(e => e.textContent.includes("leftover rice"))'`.
  - `asr_speech_start` and `asr_speech_complete` appear in `fakes.jsonl`.
- **Spoken reply.** Wait for the reply.
  - Run `$V reply $R "leftover rice. That is all."`.
  - `fakes.jsonl` has one `tts_request` per sentence, with
    `"voice": "fake_heart"`: `Fake reply to: What should I cook with leftover
    rice.` and `That is all.`
- **Stop.** Queue `Tell me a long story about bread`, wait for `speaking`, and
  tap the reticle.
  - Run `$V say $R "Tell me a long story about bread"`, `$V state $R speaking`,
    `$V ab $R click 'button[aria-label="Stop"]'` and `$V state $R listening`.
  - The Thalvi turn is cut short, and `fakes.jsonl` gains `tts_cancelled`.
- **Barge-in.** Queue two lines and let the second interrupt the first.
  - Run `$V say $R "Another long story please"`,
    `$V say $R "Actually never mind"` and `$V state $R speaking`.
  - Within one clip loop, a user turn `Actually never mind` appears, the long
    reply stops, and `fakes.jsonl` shows `asr_speech_start` followed by
    `llm_cancelled`.
  - `$V reply $R "Actually never mind. That is all."` passes.
- **Voice off.** Tap the header toggle.
  - Run `$V ab $R click 'button.o-voice'` and `$V state $R idle`.
  - `aria-pressed="false"`, and `fakes.jsonl` gains `asr_close`.
- **Proof.** Record the stop and barge-in steps with
  `$V ab $R record start $R/evidence/voice.webm` and `record stop`. Keep a
  screenshot taken during `speaking` and the `fakes.jsonl` lines.

## Gotchas

- With the recognizer queue empty, the fake ignores speech and logs
  `asr_ignored_speech`. No turn ever arrives. Queue first.
- The microphone clip loops every 8.5 s. Every queued line is spoken in the
  next burst, even mid-reply, which is what makes barge-in work but also
  surprises a recipe that queued two lines by accident.
- Chromium's noise suppression stays on, as for a user. A pure sine tone gets
  suppressed, so keep `mic.wav` as generated by `fake_endpoints.py wav`.
- Headless Chromium plays audio to a null sink. The proof that a reply was
  spoken is the `tts_request` lines and the `speaking` state, not sound.
- After `Stop` with voice on, the state is `listening`, not `idle`.
- Voice turns start from `listening`, so `$V state $R listening` passes before
  the turn is heard. Wait with `reply`.
